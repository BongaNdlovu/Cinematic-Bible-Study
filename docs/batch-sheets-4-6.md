## s4-1 — Nebuchadnezzar's Progressive Encounters with God

### step 1 — Acknowledges God as "a revealer of secrets"
- share: The same man speaks three times, and only the last costs him his glory
- ahead: This confession costs him nothing, and it does not outlast the chapter. The same court that hears it will later be ordered to worship an image, and the king who said it will be the one giving the order.

### step 2 — Acknowledges "no other God that can deliver after this sort"
- share: The three Hebrews speak words of faith, and the king answers with a decree of his own
- ahead: The words of faith here belong to the three Hebrews, not to the king; his own verdict is the decree of Daniel 3:29. That decree still leaves the image he set up standing in the plain of Dura, so the movement ends in policy rather than repentance.

## s4-3 — The Threefold Boast of Pride

### step 1 — "I have built"
- share: The same claim is made three times over, growing to take the glory as well
- ahead: In Daniel 2:37 the same prophet told this king that God had given him a kingdom, power, and strength, and glory. This clause is the boast's mirror of that gift; the next one turns from what was received to the strength that received it.

### step 2 — "By the might of my power"
- share: One man's arm is claimed first, and one man's majesty last
- ahead: The kingdom that Daniel 4:17 says the Most High gives to whomsoever he will is claimed here as the work of one man's arm. The boast goes further by taking the glory too, which is where the third movement arrives.

## s5-2 — History: The Babylonian Co-Regency Hierarchy

### step 1 — Nabonidus
- share: The co-regency puts two rulers above the post Daniel is offered
- ahead: History: the Nabonidus Chronicle places the king in Tayma while the city is governed in his name, and the Verse Account agrees. That absence leaves the throne room to a second man, so the next movement is the son ruling inside Babylon in his father's place.

### step 2 — Belshazzar
- share: Nabonidus and Belshazzar stand above Daniel, who is offered the third place
- ahead: The eldest son, co-regent ruling inside Babylon, offers the third place in the kingdom, not the second (History). In Daniel 5:7 the reward is to be the third ruler, which is what a son ruling under an absent father could actually grant. The man who receives that offer is the exile the queen remembers to the king.
