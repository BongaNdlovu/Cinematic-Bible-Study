## 1. The 1,260-Year Prophetic Epoch (538 A.D. to 1798 A.D.)

### The Biblical Span
- Daniel 7:25: "a time and times and the dividing of time" (1 + 2 + ½ = 3½ prophetic times = 1,260 prophetic days).
- Revelation 12:6, 14: "a thousand two hundred and threescore days" = "a time, and times, and half a time".
- Revelation 13:5: "forty and two months" (42 × 30 = 1,260 prophetic days).
- Application of Year-Day Principle (Num 14:34; Ezek 4:6): 1,260 prophetic days = 1,260 solar years of ecclesiastical-political supremacy.

### The Historical Anchor: 538 A.D.
1. **Justinian’s Imperial Decrees (533–534 A.D.):**
   - Emperor Justinian I issued an imperial letter to Pope John II in March 533 A.D. (incorporated into the *Codex Justinianus*, Book I, Title 1, Law 8), recognizing the Bishop of Rome as "head of all the holy churches" (*caput omnium sanctarum ecclesiarum*).
   - This legal status was confirmed and enacted in civil law by Justinian’s *Novellae* (Novel 131, Chapter 2, promulgated in 545 A.D.): "The most holy Pope of old Rome is first of all priests."
2. **The Military Clearance: Belisarius & the Ostrogothic Siege of Rome (537–538 A.D.):**
   - The Ostrogoths (Arian kingdom holding Italy) besieged General Belisarius in Rome for one year and nine days (March 537 to March 538 A.D.).
   - In March 538 A.D., King Witiges and the Ostrogothic army broke the siege and retreated north, permanently lifting Arian military control over the city of Rome and allowing the papal-imperial administration to function unhindered.
   - Primary Source: Procopius of Caesarea, *History of the Wars* (Books V–VI: *The Gothic War*).

### The Historical Terminal: 1798 A.D.
1. **General Berthier's Entry into Rome (February 15, 1798):**
   - French Revolutionary General Louis-Alexandre Berthier entered Rome with the French army.
   - On February 15, 1798, Berthier proclaimed the Roman Republic from the Capitol, formally abolishing the temporal sovereignty of the Pope.
2. **Captivity of Pope Pius VI:**
   - Pope Pius VI was arrested on February 20, 1798, stripped of temporal authority, and taken as a prisoner of state to Siena, Florence, Briançon, and finally Valence, France, where he died in captivity on August 29, 1799.
   - Primary Source: *The Times* (London), March 12, 1798; *Annual Register for the Year 1798* (London: Otridge and Son, 1800), "History of Europe," pp. 139–147; *Encyclopaedia Britannica* (11th ed., 1911), Vol. 21, "Pius VI," pp. 685–686.
3. **The Exact Duration:**
   - 538 A.D. + 1,260 years = 1798 A.D.
   - The sitting explicitly hedges this calculation as the historicist chronological synthesis, showing the documentation and historical witnesses rather than presenting the dates as printed in the verse.
