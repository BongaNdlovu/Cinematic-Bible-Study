## 1. The Reformation Consensus on Historicism & the Antichrist

### The Claim
The Protestant Reformers unanimously utilized the continuous historical (historicist) method and identified the medieval ecclesiastical-political power (the papacy) with the Little Horn of Daniel 7 and the Antichrist of 2 Thessalonians 2 and Revelation 13.

### Primary Sources
1. **Martin Luther (1483–1546):**
   - *Smalcald Articles* (1537), Part II, Art. IV:
     > "This teaching shows forcefully that the Pope is the very Antichrist, who has exalted himself above, and opposed himself against Christ, because he will not permit Christians to be saved without his power..." (Concordia: *The Lutheran Confessions*, Concordia Publishing House, 2005, p. 289).
2. **Philip Melanchthon (1497–1560):**
   - *Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope* (1537), §39:
     > "Now, it is manifest that the Roman pontiffs, with their members, defend godless doctrines and godless services. And the marks of the Antichrist plainly agree with the kingdom of the Pope and his adherents."
3. **John Calvin (1509–1564):**
   - *Institutes of the Christian Religion* (1559), Book IV, Chapter 7, Section 25:
     > "Daniel and Paul foretold that Antichrist would sit in the temple of God (Dan. ix. 27; 2 Thess. ii. 4); we regard the Roman pontiff as the leader and standard-bearer of that wicked and abominable kingdom."
4. **Sir Isaac Newton (1643–1727):**
   - *Observations upon the Prophecies of Daniel, and the Apocalypse of St. John* (London: J. Darby & T. Browne, 1733), Part I, Chapter I:
     > "Amongst the old Prophets, Daniel is most distinct in order of time, and easiest to be understood: and therefore in those things which relate to the last times, he must be made the key to the rest." (Project Gutenberg eBook #16878).

## 2. Counter-Reformation Origin of Systematic Preterism & Futurism

### The Claim
During the Counter-Reformation (Council of Trent, 1545–1563), Spanish Jesuit scholars published alternative hermeneutical systems that relocated prophetic fulfillment away from the Christian era to escape Protestant historicist identification:
- **Luis de Alcázar (1554–1613):** First systematic Preterist commentary (*Vestigatio Arcani Sensus in Apocalypsi*, published posthumously 1614), confining Daniel and Revelation to ancient Antiochus IV and Nero / A.D. 70.
- **Francisco Ribera (1537–1591):** First systematic Futurist commentary (*In Sacrum Beati Ioannis Apostoli, & Evangelistæ Apocalypsin Commentarii*, Salamanca, 1590), severing the 70th week of Daniel 9 and positing a literal 3½-year individual Antichrist in the distant future.

### Historical Documentation
- Le Roy Edwin Froom, *The Prophetic Faith of Our Fathers: The Historical Development of Prophetic Interpretation* (Washington, D.C.: Review and Herald, 1948), Vol. 2, pp. 486–532:
  - "The Preterist View of Alcazar": In 1614 Alcázar’s 900-page work appeared, arguing Revelation was accomplished in the early centuries (fall of Judaism in A.D. 70 and pagan Rome).
  - "Ribera Launches the Futurist System": In 1590 Francisco Ribera published his 500-page commentary on the Apocalypse, assigning the first few chapters to ancient Rome and throwing the rest into the distant end of time, restricting Antichrist to a single person reigning 3½ years.
- George Eldon Ladd, *The Blessed Hope* (Eerdmans, 1956), pp. 37–40: Documents the Jesuit Counter-Reformation origin of futurism and its subsequent adoption by Edward Irving and John Nelson Darby in the 1830s.

## 3. Porphyry & the Maccabean Late-Date Thesis

### The Claim
The 3rd-century pagan Neo-Platonist philosopher Porphyry of Tyre (c. 234–305 A.D.) argued in his twelfth book *Against the Christians* that Daniel was a Maccabean forgery written in the 160s B.C. after the events occurred. St. Jerome documented and refuted this thesis in his 5th-century Latin commentary.

### Primary Source
- **St. Jerome (c. 347–420 A.D.):**
  - *Commentary on Daniel* (c. 407 A.D.), Prologue (English translation by Gleason L. Archer, Jr., Grand Rapids: Baker, 1958; online at tertullian.org/fathers/jerome_daniel_02_text.htm):
    > "Porphyry wrote his twelfth book against the prophecy of Daniel, denying that it was composed by the person to whom it is ascribed in its title, but rather by some individual living in Judaea at the time of the Antiochus who was surnamed Epiphanes... because whatever is told in Daniel occurred in history down to the time of Antiochus, whereas whatever seems to be foretold beyond that point is falsehood, since he could not know the future."
  - Jerome extensively refutes Porphyry by demonstrating that the fourth kingdom of Daniel 2 and Daniel 7 is the Roman Empire, which held dominion during Jerome's own day and which Antiochus Epiphanes never attained.

## 4. The Biblical Day-for-Year Principle

### The Claim
The year-day principle is not human invention, but an explicit judicial and symbolic appointment in Scripture:
- Numbers 14:34: "After the number of the days in which ye searched the land, even forty days, each day for a year, shall ye bear your iniquities, even forty years."
- Ezekiel 4:6: "I have appointed thee each day for a year."
- Daniel 9:24–27: 70 weeks (490 prophetic days) self-checks the principle at the ministry and cross of Jesus Christ, as 490 literal days cannot rebuild Jerusalem and span to Messiah.
