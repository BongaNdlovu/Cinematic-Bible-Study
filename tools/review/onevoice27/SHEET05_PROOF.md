## 1. The Historical Existence & Co-Regency of Belshazzar

### The Contested Claim
For centuries, 19th-century rationalist critics maintained that Daniel 5 was fictitious because Greek historians (Herodotus, Xenophon, Berossus) named Nabonidus (*Nabonaid*) as the last king of Babylon, with no mention of any king named "Belshazzar."

### Archaeological & Primary Source Proof
1. **The Nabonidus Cylinder of Ur (British Museum BM 91125):**
   - Discovered by J. G. Taylor in 1854 in the ruins of the ziggurat of Ur.
   - Cuneiform inscription from King Nabonidus containing a direct prayer for his eldest son:
     > "As for me, Nabonidus, king of Babylon, save me from sinning against your great godhead... And as for Belshazzar, the eldest son, my offspring, instill reverence for your great godhead in his heart and may he not commit any sin, may he enjoy a fullness of life."
2. **The Verse Account of Nabonidus (British Museum BM 38299):**
   - Documents Nabonidus’s 10-year self-exile to the oasis of Tayma in northern Arabia, leaving the imperial crown and army of Babylon in the direct hands of Belshazzar:
     > "He entrusted the camp to his oldest son, the firstborn, the troops through the country he ordered under his command. He let everything go, entrusted the kingship to him, and, himself, he started out on a long journey."
3. **The Biblical Harmony in Daniel 5:7, 16, 29:**
   - Belshazzar offers to make Daniel "the third ruler in the kingdom" (*talti* or *teltiy* in Aramaic, Dan 5:7, 29).
   - Daniel could not be made the second ruler because Nabonidus was first, and Belshazzar was second as co-regent. Only the third position was available to be conferred.
   - Reference: Raymond P. Dougherty, *Nabonidus and Belshazzar: A Study of the Closing Events of the Neo-Babylonian Empire* (Yale University Press, 1929).
