## Alignment note

- **Matches BRI:**
  - Exile defined as covenant judicial displacement (Deut 28; Jer 25; SDABC 4:756; ABSG Q1 2020 Lesson 2).
  - The 605, 597, and 586 B.C. deportations are kept scrupulously distinct, preventing the conflation of Daniel 1 with Jehoiachin's capture (attested in Babylonian Chronicle BM 21946) or the 586 burning of Solomon's temple.
  - The defilement line at the royal table is grounded on covenant worship, sacrificial table-fellowship with idols (1 Cor 10:18–21), and Leviticus 11 clean/unclean distinctions, rather than modern secular diet fads.
  - The civic/worship boundary is drawn cleanly: Daniel and his companions mastered secular Chaldean statecraft, cuneiform literature, and public administration without covenant surrender.
  - Christology links Daniel's refusal directly to Christ's victory in the wilderness of temptation (Matt 4:1–10).
  - Enrichments noted in ABSG Q1 2020 Lesson 2 are integrated: Shinar as Genesis 11:2 Babel echo, and Daniel 1:21 ("unto the first year of king Cyrus") as the restoration horizon.
- **Goes beyond BRI:**
  - The Genesis 1:29 Edenic-diet parallel is a distinctive Adventist health-message connection; the lesson correctly labels it as a literary and theological "echo" while explicitly guarding against any magical view of vegetables ("God, not the vegetables, gave them wisdom").
  - The citation of Xenophon, *Cyropaedia* 1.3.10 adds classical Persian court evidence for the first-portion offering custom, properly cited as historical context.
- **Translation labeling:**
  - All Scripture quotes in `SHEET_VERIFY[1]` and the article's load-bearing pull-quotes match the King James Version line-for-line.
