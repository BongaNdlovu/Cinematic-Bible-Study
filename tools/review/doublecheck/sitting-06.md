## 3. Sitting-Specific Probes (Plan §1.4)

### Probe 6.1: Identification of "Darius the Mede" (Dan 5:31; 6:1)
- **Lesson Presentation:** The callout box `"First principles • Who is Darius the Mede?"` candidly states: *"Daniel 5:31 and 6:1 introduce 'Darius the Mede' as the man who 'received the kingdom' at about sixty-two. Greek historians name Cyrus as the conqueror; they do not list a Median 'Darius' on the Persian throne. This study does not hide that gap."* It presents three scholarly readings:
  1. Ugbaru/Gubaru, the general who took Babylon and appointed governors under Cyrus.
  2. A Median colleague/sub-king of Cyrus during the initial transitional year (matching Xenophon's Cyaxares II).
  3. Cyrus the Great himself using a Median title / throne-name (appositional translation of Dan 6:28: "in the reign of Darius, even in the reign of Cyrus the Persian").
- **SDA Historicist Evaluation:** Fully endorsed by SDABC Vol. 4, pp. 814–817 and Dr. William H. Shea. Historicist scholarship treats Darius the Mede as a historical reality while acknowledging the present incompleteness of cuneiform/secular titling correlations. Centering the lesson on the theological and legal test rather than a speculative modern ID card is sound pedagogy.

### Probe 6.2: Constitutional Irrevocability of Medo-Persian Law
- **Lesson Presentation:** Contrasts Babylonian autocracy with Medo-Persian legal immutability (*"the law of the Medes and Persians, which does not alter"*, Dan 6:8, 12, 15). Corroborates this via Esther 1:19 and 8:8 (Ahasuerus unable to cancel the extermination decree, requiring a counter-decree) and Diodorus Siculus XVII.30 (Darius III unable to reverse a capital sentence).
- **SDA Historicist Evaluation:** Verified. SDA commentaries (SDABC Vol. 3 on Esther; Vol. 4 on Daniel 6) cite Esther 1:19 and 8:8 as the primary biblical companion text illustrating the unbending nature of Persian royal decrees. Diodorus Siculus XVII.30 is an established classical historical parallel.

### Probe 6.3: Theology of Civil Disobedience & the First Commandment
- **Lesson Presentation:** Explains that Daniel's refusal to obey the 30-day prayer ban was not political subversion, but obedience to the First Commandment ("Thou shalt have no other gods before me"). When human statutes command what God forbids or forbid what God commands, civil disobedience is a sacred obligation (Acts 5:29: *"We ought to obey God rather than men"*). Crucially, the diagnostic in Quiz 1 and the narrative body cite Dan 6:22 (*"before thee, O king, have I done no hurt"*), proving Daniel remained a completely loyal, non-violent civil servant.
- **SDA Historicist Evaluation:** Verified. This aligns exactly with Seventh-day Adventist religious liberty principles and Ellen G. White's explicit instruction (*The Acts of the Apostles*, pp. 68–69; *Prophets and Kings*, pp. 542–546). Daniel did not organize a revolt or break civil laws indiscriminately; he peacefully submitted to the penalty while refusing to compromise his direct worship of Yahweh.

### Probe 6.4: Facing Jerusalem & Solomon's Temple Dedication
- **Lesson Presentation:** Explains that Daniel's practice of kneeling toward Jerusalem three times daily was not local superstition or magical folklore, but covenantal obedience grounded in King Solomon's dedication prayer for the temple (1 Kings 8:46–50; 2 Chron 6:36–39) and Israel's daily prayer rhythm (Ps 55:17).
- **SDA Historicist Evaluation:** Verified. SDABC Vol. 4 on Dan 6:10 explicitly references 1 Kings 8:44, 48 and Ps 55:17 as the scriptural foundation for exilic prayer facing the sanctuary.

### Probe 6.5: Typology of Daniel in the Pit to Christ in the Tomb
- **Lesson Presentation:** The Christology pane and Quiz 2 frame Daniel's ordeal (condemnation by envious leaders, pit covered by a stone, sealed by the royal signet, miraculous morning emergence unharmed, vindication by the king) as a vivid prophetic type of Jesus Christ's burial and resurrection (Matt 27:62–66; Heb 13:20; 1 Pet 5:8).
- **SDA Historicist Evaluation:** Verified. SDA homiletics and theological exposition (e.g., C. Mervyn Maxwell, *God Cares*, Vol. 1, p. 102; Uriah Smith, *Daniel and the Revelation*, pp. 119–120) treat Daniel's pit deliverance as a striking Old Testament type of Christ's resurrection from the sealed Roman tomb, without treating it as an apocalyptic time prophecy.
