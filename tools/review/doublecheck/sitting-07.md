## 3. Sitting-Specific Probes (Plan §1.4)

### Probe 7.1: Celestial Court vs. Terrestrial Stone (Two Distinct Reckonings)
- **Lesson Presentation:** The callout box `"First principles • Court vs. stone: Two distinct moments of reckoning"` and Quiz 2 make the decisive theological distinction:
  - In Daniel 2, the stone strikes the feet of the image at the Second Advent, pulverizing earthly kingdoms.
  - In Daniel 7, a celestial court convenes in *heaven* before the Ancient of Days long before the stone strikes (7:9–10). The court sits *while the little horn is still speaking on earth* (7:11).
  - In Daniel 7:13, the Son of Man comes with the clouds of heaven *TO* the Ancient of Days in heaven ("they brought Him near before Him") to receive the kingdom and advocate for the saints—NOT a descent to the earth.
  - Only *after* this pre-advent forensic examination of the record books does the execution of the sentence occur on earth (7:26–27).
- **SDA Historicist Evaluation:** This is the cornerstone of Seventh-day Adventist eschatology and Fundamental Belief 24. SDABC Vol. 4 (pp. 828–831) and DARCOM emphasize that Daniel 7:9–14 portrays the opening of the pre-advent investigative judgment in the heavenly sanctuary, answering the blasphemies of the little horn.

### Probe 7.2: Derivation and Building of the 1,260 Years
- **Lesson Presentation:** The callout box `"First principles • How 7:25 becomes 1,260 years"` instructs students: *"Do not memorize 1,260 years first. Build the number."*
  1. A "time" = 1 year, "times" = 2 years, "the dividing of time" = 1/2 year $\rightarrow$ $1 + 2 + 0.5 = 3.5$ years.
  2. Revelation equates this span: "a thousand two hundred and threescore days" (Rev 12:6), "a time, and times, and half a time" (Rev 12:14), and "forty and two months" (Rev 13:5). 42 months $\times$ 30 days = 1,260 prophetic days.
  3. Applied to the year-day scale (Num 14:34; Ezek 4:6), 1,260 days become 1,260 solar years.
- **SDA Historicist Evaluation:** Flawless scriptural demonstration. Follows the standard historicist methodology published by the BRI and DARCOM.

### Probe 7.3: Candidate Historical Boundaries (538 to 1798 A.D.)
- **Lesson Presentation:** The lesson presents A.D. 538 to A.D. 1798 as the historicist candidate path rather than a dogmatic proof-text printed in the Bible:
  - *Start (538 A.D.):* When the Ostrogothic siege of Rome was raised by Belisarius, breaking the third Arian power that stood in the way of a church-state power, Justinian's earlier legal grant (533 A.D.) recognizing the bishop of Rome as the head of all churches could actively operate in the city.
  - *End (1798 A.D.):* French General Louis-Alexandre Berthier entered Rome, abolished the papal government, proclaimed the Roman Republic, and took Pope Pius VI into exile (the "deadly wound" of Rev 13:3).
  - *Honesty Statement:* *"You are allowed to test those dates. They are not magic labels placed on the verse after the fact without a path... You may argue the start-year. You may not skip the marks in 7:24–25 and still claim any favorite villain."*
- **SDA Historicist Evaluation:** Verified. SDABC Vol. 4 on Dan 7:25 and DARCOM Vol. 1 treat 538 and 1798 as historical anchors while recognizing that the legal and military processes developed over time.

### Probe 7.4: The Bear's Three Ribs Labeling
- **Lesson Presentation:** The beast table notes: *"3. Bear Raised on One Side: Medo-Persia • Three ribs in mouth (the traditional Adventist identification: Lydia, Babylon, Egypt — see the SDA Bible Commentary on 7:5 and the Adult Bible Study Guide, Daniel, 2020 Q1, week 8)."*

This completely honors the fix applied in the 2026-09-13 pass, correctly identifying the traditional interpretation of the ribs as the three kingdoms conquered by Medo-Persia (Lydia 547 B.C., Babylon 539 B.C., Egypt 525 B.C.) without overclaiming biblical dogmatism.

### Probe 7.5: Tone, Office-Level Framing, and Disclosure of Newton's Triple
- **Lesson Presentation:**
  - The little horn is framed strictly as an institutional office: *"identifies this horn with the papacy—the office of the bishop of Rome exercising both spiritual and civil authority over the fragments of the Roman world"* (never attacking the personal faith of individual believers).
