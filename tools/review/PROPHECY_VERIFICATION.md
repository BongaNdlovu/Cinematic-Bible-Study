## Corrections applied to the course text

1. **Pius VI's death.** The text ran Berthier's February 1798 entry into the
   pope's death with no year, reading as though he died in 1798. He died a
   prisoner at Valence on **29 August 1799**. Corrected.
2. **Antiochus IV's tribute to Rome.** The Apamea indemnity (188 B.C.) fell on
   his father **Antiochus III**; Antiochus IV had been a Roman hostage.
   Corrected to say so, which serves the argument better.
3. **"Three decisive grounds" vs four listed criteria.** Count corrected, and
   the set now says which three are the study guide's and which is the course's.
4. **"God explicitly commands that a prophetic day represents a literal solar
   year."** Stronger than the church's own materials. Reworded so the
   symbolic-vision logic carries the principle, as BRI's primary argument does.
5. **The Edson cornfield claim.** Stated flatly in the sitting; Andrews
   University's own journal records no contemporary corroboration, and the
   course already hedged it in the map and glossary. Hedged here too, and the
   absolute "the earth is never called the sanctuary" softened.
6. **"Catechism numbering that omits the second commandment."** The Catholic and
   Lutheran numbering folds the graven-image clause into the first commandment
   rather than deleting one. Corrected to "folds… into".
7. **Ribera "1590".** Sources give about 1590, 1591, or the 1593 Lyon edition.
   Corrected to "c. 1590" with the variants noted.
8. **"Counter-Reformation" as the sole origin label.** Qualified: neither was
   the first to read prophecy that way, but each was the first to make it a
   systematic alternative to historicism.
9. **"The 1,260-year temporal supremacy."** SDABC warns "no dates can be given
   to mark a sharp transition… the rise and fall of the papacy were both
   gradual developments." The 1798 claim now says so.
10. **Candour on the Antiochus reading.** The text now states plainly that the
    majority of modern scholarship reads Daniel 8 of Antiochus, and that the
    historicist reading is a minority conclusion which this course's own
    tradition acknowledges as such.

## What a reviewer is most likely to challenge — and the honest state of each

| Item | State |
|---|---|
| **A.D. 31** | Standard, but on confessional grounds — the "midst of the 70th week" from autumn 27 — **not** astronomical proof. SDABC says the case "cannot be established by astronomical and calendrical proof" and that A.D. 30 is more popular among scholars generally. The text does not claim otherwise, but a reviewer will raise it. |
| **A.D. 34 / Stephen** | Standard identification, but SDABC says there is **no direct historical evidence** for the date of Stephen's death; 34 is a "working date". Not yet hedged in the text. |
| **457 B.C.** | Standard within historicism; outside it, most critical scholarship dates Ezra under Artaxerxes II or Artaxerxes I's 37th year, and SDABC concedes the camps are "about equal in number". Also an internal 458/457 two-reckoning question the text does not address. |
| **508 / 1,290 / 1,335** | Standard Adventist reading (BRI calls it "the historical position… up to our own days"), and 1843 is the terminus in SDABC, the study guide and Uriah Smith. But it **depends on reading the *tamid* as paganism**, and SDABC says of the rival reading — the one this course takes elsewhere — that it can "find no satisfactory explanation of this text". **This tension is unresolved in the course and is the single most likely thing a well-read reviewer will press.** |
| **538 as the 1,260 start** | An official church paper concedes it is a backward calculation from 1798 and that the 538 event was "anticlimactic". |
| **"Four Persian decrees"** | The official study guide uses **three** (Cyrus, Darius, Artaxerxes). The fourth (Nehemiah's 444 commission) is a common but non-standard framing. |

## Terminology note for the committee

The phrase "Investigative Judgment" appears **zero** times in the course. This is
enforced by the project's own script (`tools/verify/verify_onevoice27.mjs`), which
treats it as a forbidden learner-facing brand. The doctrine is taught in full
under the names "pre-Advent judgment" and "the pre-Advent heavenly court".

The committee should know that **"investigative judgment" is the church's own
term** — it is in the title of BRI's article on the subject, in the official
study guide, and in the DARCOM report. Avoiding it is a stylistic decision, not a
doctrinal one. If the committee prefers, one bracketed clause would remove any
appearance of concealment.
